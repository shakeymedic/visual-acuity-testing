General Eye Assessment Protocol

1. External Inspection

Examine the orbit, eyelids, conjunctiva, and sclera. Check for erythema, swelling, or discharge. Evert the upper eyelid to look for hidden foreign bodies. Assess the anterior chamber for hyphaema or hypopyon.

2. Pupils

Note size, shape, and symmetry. Test direct and consensual light reflexes. Perform the swinging flashlight test to check for a Relative Afferent Pupillary Defect.

3. Eye Movements

Ask the patient to follow your finger in an 'H' pattern. Test all nine directions of gaze. Ask the patient if they experience double vision or pain during movement.

4. Visual Fields

Assess by confrontation. Sit level with the patient. Ask them to cover one eye and look directly at your nose. Test all four quadrants using finger movements.

5. Fluorescein Staining

Instil fluorescein drops and examine the cornea using a cobalt blue light. Look for abrasions, dendritic ulcers, or fluid leaking from the anterior chamber.

6. Red Flags

Escalate immediately if you note sudden visual loss, severe pain, chemical exposure, an irregular pupil, or a suspected penetrating globe injury.
